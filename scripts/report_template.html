Change detection · Sentinel-2 · no training

Training-free change detection on two sites: Tomisato (Chiba) and SNCF Bassin

Four unsupervised methods — NDVI difference, Change Vector Analysis, IR-MAD, and TESSERA foundation-model embeddings — applied to season-matched Sentinel-2 pairs. A pixel is called changed when at least two methods agree.

What was run

Imagery comes from the public Earth Search STAC API (Sentinel-2 L2A COGs on AWS) — same data as the internal GeoData-APIs script but with no keys required; scenes are listed as a GeoDataFrame, one index is picked per date, and the four 10 m RGBI bands are windowed straight out of the cloud-optimized GeoTIFFs, snapped to a common 10 m grid so both dates align pixel-perfectly.

Methods (all training-free)

  • ΔNDVI Vegetation index difference after robust per-band radiometric normalization. Directional: red = vegetation loss.
  • CVA Change Vector Analysis — Euclidean magnitude of the 4-band spectral change vector; Otsu threshold.
  • IR-MAD Iteratively Reweighted Multivariate Alteration Detection (Nielsen 2007) — canonical-correlation change statistic, invariant to linear radiometric differences between acquisitions; Otsu threshold on the χ² statistic.
  • TESSERA Cambridge's TESSERA foundation model: precomputed 128-dim annual embeddings of the full Sentinel-1/2 time series at 10 m, fetched via geotessera. Change = per-pixel cosine distance between years. Because each embedding summarizes a whole year of observations, it is insensitive to single-date artifacts (clouds, sun angle, one-off field states) — the strongest "potential solution" of the set.

Site 1 — Shimizu / Tomisato, Chiba (2020 → 2025)

Top: before/after true-color, consensus change overlay (pink), method agreement count. Bottom: ΔNDVI, CVA magnitude, IR-MAD √χ², TESSERA cosine distance. Cyan outline = AOI.

The methods converge on a set of discrete parcels: new large-roof buildings (warehouses/greenhouses) in the south-central and eastern AOI, cleared and newly developed lots along the northern edge, and field-to-built conversions. Agricultural parcels rotate crops between the two springs, which single-date methods partially flag; the 2-of-3 consensus suppresses most of that noise while keeping the construction footprints. TESSERA shows the same hotspots with sharper parcel boundaries, plus broader agricultural-practice change that annual embeddings legitimately see.

The dark-red block in the agreement map is that development; the ΔNDVI panel shows it as vegetation loss.

Assessment of the approaches

  • TESSERA is the most promising single method for this use case: no training, 10 m resolution, whole-year robustness, and simple to productionize (fetch two years of embeddings, cosine distance, threshold). Main limits today are coverage lag (Japan 2025 not yet published; 2026 not yet available anywhere) and no sub-annual dating of the change.
  • IR-MAD + CVA on season-matched pairs is a strong, fully self-contained fallback that works on any pair of dates (including the freshest imagery), at the cost of sensitivity to phenology and single-date artifacts.
  • Consensus voting (≥2 of 3) gave the cleanest maps on both sites and is what the pink overlays show.
  • For AOIs as small as Bassin (~800 pixels), Sentinel-2 is near its useful floor — detected change is credible but coarse; VHR imagery would be needed for structure-level detail.

Operational monitoring backtest (alerting tiers)

Beyond the one-shot pair analysis, a monitoring pipeline was backtested: every usable Sentinel-2 scene over the AOI is cloud-masked with the L2A scene classification (SCL) and compared against a monthly baseline composite (median of the same month ±1 across the 3 prior years). A pixel raises an alert only after being anomalous (robust z > 4) on 3 consecutive observations — the persistence rule that suppresses clouds, shadows and one-off field states. Two detectors run side by side: Tier 1 CVA on raw spectra, and Tier 2 dense deep features from SSL4EO's Sentinel-2-pretrained ResNet18 (TorchGeo), compared by cosine distance.

Chiba — monitored through 2025 (58 scenes, baseline 2022–2024)

Left: alerts on the after-image, colored by first-alert date. Middle: cumulative alerted area with per-scene new alerts. Right: raw per-scene anomaly fraction before persistence.

Tier 1 accumulated 131 ha of alerts, mostly crop-rotation confetti — in agriculture, this year's fields genuinely differ from the baseline for months, so persistence alone cannot suppress it. Tier 2 alerted 49 ha in compact parcel-shaped blobs and a ~2.7× lower per-scene anomaly rate. Both tiers agree on a real burst of change in mid-November 2025. With ~5–10 usable scenes/month, the 3-observation rule gives a detection latency of roughly 1–3 weeks.

Same layout. The initial August-2025 burst is carry-over change (the area was already mid-transformation relative to the older baseline); fresh alert waves appear in March and June 2026.

The winter cadence gap (few usable optical scenes November–January) is visible in the right-hand panels — that is the slot Sentinel-1 radar would fill in a production system.

SSL4EO pairwise change maps (two-date, deep features)

The same SSL4EO features also give a TESSERA-style two-date change map that works for any pair of dates (no embedding-coverage lag): cosine distance between the dense features of the two scenes, thresholded at robust z > 4. Deep-feature maps are blobbier than spectral ones (receptive field ≈ a few hundred metres) but far less sensitive to phenology.
